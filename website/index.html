Offshore in-situ testing

CPTu thrust mitigation in glauconite sand

Authigenic glauconite sands exhibit severe grain crushing and dilative negative pore pressures that cause premature refusal during offshore site investigations. Within the Piling in Glauconitic Sand (PIGS) Joint Industry Project, our field program evaluated six CPTu configurations in the Hornerstown and Navesink formations.

Key result48 mm friction reducers with 6–7 L/min water jetting extended penetration from ≈14 m to 23.5 m.

Read the paper

Deep foundations

Calibration-chamber modeling of pile setup

Designed and instrumented a pressurized consolidation calibration chamber and a miniature model pile (3 piezometers, 3 strain gauges) to decouple excess pore-water pressure (EPWP) dissipation from long-term thixotropic aging in kaolinite clay at OCR 1.2 and 4.8.

Key resultEPWP dissipation (< 3 days) raised shaft friction by 287%; aging (> 3 days) added a further 28.8%.

Particle morphology

3D particle sphericity via sequential sieving

Developed a multi-time-step sequential sieving method and empirical relations linking 2D optical projection circularity to true 3D sphericity indices and Zingg flatness and elongation ratios for irregular fine granular materials.

Key resultBenchmarked and validated against 3D X-ray micro-CT tomography.

Journal articles

  1. 2026 Journal article

    Mitigation of CPT thrust force through glauconite sand

    Westgate, Z. J., Senanayake, A., Shafiei, M., & DeGroot, D. J.

    Canadian Geotechnical Journal · doi:10.1139/cgj-2026-0117

    • 6 CPTu configurations
    • Hornerstown & Navesink glauconite
    • 50–80% thrust reduction
    Publisher (DOI) ResearchGate
    Summary

    Investigated methods to increase piezocone penetration (CPTu) depth in authigenic glauconite sand deposits (Hornerstown and Navesink formations) as part of the Piling in Glauconitic Sand (PIGS) Joint Industry Project at the Search Farm test site, New Jersey. While standard friction reducers cut rod thrust by 50–80%, enlarged 48 mm friction reducers combined with upper-bevel water jetting (4 × 3 mm ports at 6–7 L/min) effectively mitigated rod friction and negative pore-pressure build-up, extending penetration from ≈14 m to 23.5 m.

  2. 2024 Journal article

    Quantifying three-dimensional sphericity indices of irregular fine particles from 2D images through sequential sieving tests

    Kaviani-Hamedani, F., Esmailzade, M., Adineh, K., Shafiei, M., & Shirkavand, D.

    Granular Matter, 26(1), Article 10 · doi:10.1007/s10035-023-01380-z

    • 3D X-ray micro-CT benchmark
    • Zingg aspect ratios
    • Sequential sieving
    Publisher (DOI) ResearchGate
    Summary

    Formulated a multi-time-step sequential sieving test method and developed empirical predictive equations relating 2D optical projection circularities to true 3D particle sphericity indices and Zingg flatness and elongation ratios. Benchmarked against 3D X-ray micro-CT tomography across irregular sand grains and seven synthetic shapes, showing that 3D image-processing grading curves align closely with mechanical sieve analysis.

  3. 2023 Journal article

    Investigation of soil setup effects on pile response in clay considering overconsolidation ratio and installation method through physical modeling

    Fakharian, K., Shafiei, M., & Hafezan, S.

    Canadian Geotechnical Journal, 60(4), 488–503 · doi:10.1139/cgj-2021-0686

    • 21 static load tests
    • OCR 1.2 & 4.8 clay
    • EPWP vs. thixotropic aging
    Publisher (DOI)
    Summary

    Constructed a pressurized calibration chamber and a custom instrumented CPT-scale model pile with three miniature piezometers and three strain gauges. Executed 21 static load tests in kaolinite clay at OCR 1.2 (normally consolidated) and OCR 4.8 (overconsolidated) from 1 hour to 90 days. Separated the contribution of excess pore-water pressure dissipation (< 3 days: +287% shaft friction for the driven pile in NC clay) from secondary soil aging (> 3 days: +28.8%), and formulated a quantitative relation for the evolution of pile stiffness.
