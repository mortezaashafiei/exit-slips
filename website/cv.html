Selected research projects

Particle degradation of glauconite sand (Ph.D.)

  • Hornerstown and Navesink glauconite sands (New Jersey) degraded by 1D compression to 50 MPa, ball milling, and dispersion-cup mixing, matched to the stress zones around a driven pile.
  • Showed that particle-size classification alone is insufficient: free water and stress regime control the plasticity of the fines produced.

CPTu thrust reduction in glauconite sand (PIGS Joint Industry Project)

  • Field comparison of cone size, friction-reducer length, diameter and number, and water jetting at a New Jersey site.
  • A larger 15 cm² cone, a larger-diameter reducer, and upper-bevel water jetting significantly reduced thrust (layer-specific); longer or multiple standard reducers did not.

Pile setup in clay by calibration-chamber modeling (M.Sc.)

  • 400 mm × 1000 mm consolidation chamber; CPT-size model pile with three strain gauges and three pore-pressure transducers; kaolinite at OCR 1.2 and 4.8; static load tests from 1 hour to 90 days.
  • Driven piles set up faster and reached greater long-term shaft friction than jacked-in piles; setup separated into pore-pressure dissipation and aging components.

3D particle sphericity from 2D images

  • Sequential sieving and X-ray micro-CT (3,000+ particles per material) linking 2D shape indices to 3D sphericity and Zingg flatness and elongation ratios.
